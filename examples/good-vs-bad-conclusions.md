# Good vs bad conclusions (synthetic)

Use the fixture study as a reminder of claim boundaries. None of these
sentences describe a real creator or a live platform measurement.

## Bad

> The 2026 late-summer hotspot caused home-coffee how-to posts to spike on
> Douyin, so the track is healthy and ready for brand deals.

Why it fails: hotspot association is not causation; cumulative likes are not
fixed-age performance; a tiny purposive sample is not prevalence; visible
interest is not commercial proof.

## Good

> In this 10-row synthetic fixture, how-to and comparison captions appear
> alongside both mid-range and low like counts. One Douyin demo row has a much
> higher cumulative count. That is an association inside this sample, not
> evidence that a hotspot caused a spike, and not evidence that brand
> cooperation would convert.

The good version keeps the sample boundary, names a counterexample, and refuses
A → B shortcuts listed in `references/evidence-and-claims.md`.
